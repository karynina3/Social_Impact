Hydrological SWOT Analysis

Strengths

  • GEOGLOWS services (Hydroviewer, Hydroserver, Flood Maps, Groundwater) are valuable for water management
  • Models like HBV-Light and HEC-HMS already in use for hydrological modeling in some countries
  • Operational hydrological and meteorological stations across countries
  • Data sharing agreement among NHS to improve GEOGLOWS River Forecasting System
  • Long term record data: 25-50 year historical streamflow and water level
  • Institutional priorities for improving early warning systems and water management

Weaknesses

  • Limited groundwater level historical data (1-25 years) with inconsistent records only in few countries
  • Small modeling teams (3-7 people) leading to overwork and limited scalability
  • Lack of expertise in advanced modeling and data integration techniques
  • Gaps in database systems and lack of centralized platforms for data access
  • Poor station maintenance reducing real-time data reliability
  • Undefined budgets for hydrological monitoring in many institutions

Opportunities

  • Training programs to upskill personnel in modeling and database management
  • Increased GEOGLOWS adoption through workshops and demonstration projects
  • Expanding GEOGLOWS for real-time monitoring, flood mapping, and bias correction
  • Strengthening partnerships with international organizations for support
  • Open to collaboration after october, NHS will check the budget
  • Upgrading hydrometeorological stations for consistent data

Threats

  • Inadequate financial resources for maintenance and technology adoption
  • Funding delays impacting NHS
  • Challenges integrating GEOGLOWS with existing systems
  • Dependence on external expertise for technical issues
  • GEOGLOWS Version Updates to track events during rainy season
  • Resistance to adopting new tools due to lack of awareness

Customization Apps

The GEOGLOWS River Forecasting System provides global streamflow forecasts by combining weather predictions from the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) with hydrological modeling.

  • Offers daily ensemble forecasts up to 15 days in advance
  • Provides over 80 years of historical simulated streamflow data for approximately 7 million river reaches worldwide
  • Especially valuable in data-scarce regions
  • Enables users to monitor river conditions, analyze trends, and plan for flood and drought events
  • Forecasts can be accessed through the interactive GEOGLOWS Hydroviewer
  • Available via APIs and GIS platforms like Esri's Living Atlas
  • Supports decision-making in water resource management and disaster preparedness

The GRACE Groundwater Subsetting Tool (GGST) is an open-source web application designed to make satellite-based groundwater data from NASA's GRACE mission accessible and usable, especially for stakeholders in regions with limited technical capacity.

  • Allows users to view and analyze changes in groundwater storage over time
  • Derived from total water storage anomalies using a mass balance approach with supporting GLDAS land surface model data
  • Intuitive interface enables both global and regional analyses
  • Users can visualize time-series graphs, download data, and assess trends in aquifer depletion or recharge
  • Features include animated maps, shapefile uploads for regional focus, and built-in uncertainty estimates
  • Provides a powerful decision-support tool for sustainable groundwater management
  • Particularly valuable in data-scarce or transboundary regions, where traditional groundwater monitoring is challenging or nonexistent

HydroServer is an open-source, modular platform designed for the collection, management, and sharing of hydrologic time series data from environmental monitoring sites.

  • Includes a user-friendly web interface for managing monitoring sites, variables, and metadata
  • Provides tools for data ingestion and visualization
  • Important for flood forecasting and early warning systems
  • Enables timely dissemination of critical information to vulnerable communities

The flood maps represent the extent and depth of water under different flow scenarios, using open-source tools and geospatial data.

  • Created through a raster-based modeling approach
  • Uses ARC (Automated Rating Curve) and Curve2Flood software
  • ARC calculates rating curves for each point along the river network based on topographic data (DEM), land use, and Manning's roughness coefficients
  • Curve2Flood uses these curves, along with target flow values, to quickly produce flood inundation maps
  • Requires basic inputs such as a digital elevation model, river network raster, flow data, and land use maps
  • Unlike traditional hydrodynamic models, this approach delivers useful results more efficiently and with fewer data requirements
  • Particularly valuable in data-scarce regions
